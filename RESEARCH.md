# Durga Puja experience research

## Cultural anchors

### The festival is a living installation

UNESCO's description frames Kolkata's Durga Puja as both a public performance of religion and a thriving ground for collaborative artists and designers. That led to the core product idea: the site should feel like entering a temporary city-scale installation, not reading an event listing.

### Clay, eyes, river, return

The clay idol's journey supplies the narrative spine:

1. Clay is gathered from the Ganga and shaped by artisans.
2. At Mahalaya the eyes are painted, giving the image presence.
3. The city gathers around pandals, light, food, prayer, music and art.
4. On Dashami the idol returns to the river.
5. The feeling returns home with the people.

### The five-day public crescendo

The main experience uses five “acts” rather than a schedule grid:

- Shasthi — the city dresses up.
- Saptami — the first full morning of the puja.
- Ashtami — the dense, collective pulse of the festival.
- Navami — the festival's bright social peak.
- Dashami — release, farewell and homecoming.

The UI shows five cards with a progressive move from warm dawn to afterglow. They can be read as a visual score, not a literal ritual guide.

## Sonic direction

The sound UI separates three moods:

- **Agomoni / The Calling:** the dawn and Mahalaya mood, a nod to the devotional invocation tradition.
- **Dhak / The Pulse:** the physical collective energy of pandal drumming and procession.
- **Neon Sindoor / Afterglow:** a fictional “future folk” layer for the modern, design-led theme-pandal culture.

No commercial recording is embedded. The prototype generates a small browser tone pulse so the interface can be experienced without shipping an unlicensed song.

## Digital design references

### Bruno Simon — playful world-building

The portfolio turns navigation into a place, gives the visitor a clear physical relationship to the scene, and hides information inside discovery. Adapted here as a chapter rail, ambient movement, and a ritual that can be entered rather than a standard hero carousel.

### Award-gallery / creative studio language

The reference pattern from Awwwards, The FWA, and studio portfolio sites is consistent: editorial typography, compact mono labels, strong art direction, chapter transitions, and interaction that rewards curiosity. This project borrows the grammar while keeping the cultural subject in front.

## Visual decisions

- Ink black and deep wine create the feeling of a pandal after dark.
- Saffron, lamp gold and terracotta represent sindoor, clay and fire without turning the page into a generic festival template.
- The abstract goddess mark uses eyes, a crown, a trident and a halo as symbolic forms; it is not intended to stand in for any specific idol style.
- The “leave a light” interaction translates the emotional gesture of carrying the festival home into a small digital ritual.
