# Devotional artwork and cultural references

The old procedural mask and goddess geometry have been removed completely. The revised sacred figures are original generated artwork placed as transparent planes inside the existing 3D environments. They are not volumetric scanned idols; the camera, environment, lamps, water, drums and Nabapatrika remain rendered in 3D. This preserves the painted faces and avoids stretching or deforming sacred iconography.

Generated with the built-in image-generation tool, using these production briefs:

1. **Uma and her family:** traditional Bengali ekchala painted-clay pratima; serene golden-ivory face, elongated painted eyes, third eye, red-and-cream sari, intricate white shola crown and ornaments, ten arms and traditional attributes; Lakshmi, Saraswati, Ganesha and Kartik with recognizable attributes and mounts; lion and subdued Mahishasura; full composition, warm lamp light, transparent background, no caricature or gore.
2. **Mahishasura Mardini:** original Bengali devotional battle tableau; the same gracious divine mother in a protective pose with ten arms, traditional weapons and sari, lion and buffalo-demon; a calm expression even in victory, sunrise lighting, complete figures, transparent background, no blood, horror or copied television footage.

Web assets: `uma-family.webp` and `mahishasura-mardini.webp`. Optimized WebP copies preserve the 1536 × 1024 artwork and its alpha channel. Original generated PNGs are preserved as separate deliverables.

## River and dhaki environment revision

Two additional original assets were made with the built-in image-generation tool and optimized to WebP:

- **Hooghly ghat** (`hooghly-ghat.webp`): a wide photorealistic Kolkata river scene at warm dusk, grey-brown water with irregular small ripples, weathered stone steps, small spectators in ordinary Bengali clothes, wooden boats, a distant riverfront and subtle bridge silhouette. Open water leaves room for the story. No additional idol is painted into the backdrop. The image is an imagined environment, not a photograph of a specific ghat.
- **Dhaki musicians** (`dhaki-musicians.webp`): three full-body Bengali musicians, natural human proportions, cotton dhotis and kurtas, red-and-white gamchas, barefoot stances, shoulder-supported barrel dhaks with rope lacing and feather decoration, and two slender sticks. Warm pandal lighting, transparent background, no deity or scenery in the performer layer.

The river backdrop has a localized water shimmer; the sky, boats and spectators within that artwork stay stable. Additional small 3D attendants support the pratima's wooden-and-bamboo carrying frame, with waterline clipping, while 3D ferries gently rock. Navami includes the photographic-style musician layer, two small animated 3D drummers, spectators, marigold garlands, columns, lamps and an original chalk-like floor pattern. Reduced-motion preference stops river shimmer, rocking, gestures and performer movement.

## Six-scene realism revision (13 October 2026)

All six environment plates are original generated artwork, inspired by research rather than copied photographs. People within these plates are still imagery. Water-only shimmer, light shafts, framing movement and directional dissolves are implemented in FestivalBackdrop.jsx; instanced petals and existing devotional/musician layers provide depth. Reduced motion freezes these effects. The old geometric crowd, riverbank and plant constructions were removed.

Generation briefs:
- bengal-dawn.webp: photorealistic Hooghly dawn ghat, distant priests, brass vessels, boats, kash grass and shiuli, empty foreground for the Mahishasura Mardini stage.
- thakur-dalan.webp: North Kolkata bonedi-bari courtyard at blue hour, weathered cream/vermilion arches, green shutters, iron balconies, chandeliers, marigolds, alpona, devotees at edges, empty shrine for compositing.
- saptami-ghat.webp: Nabapatrika snan with a banana plant and eight associated plants in a red-bordered white sari, priest and helpers, vessels and flowers, ordinary devotees at the ghat, natural river and dawn light.
- ashtami-anjali.webp: daylight thakur dalan with small groups cupping flowers for pushpanjali, hibiscus and bel leaves, lamps and incense, empty right altar for the family artwork.
- navami-pandal.webp: evening bamboo and terracotta community pandal, chandeliers, marigolds, alpona, families at the sides, incense and an empty performance area for the existing dhakis.
- bhasan-farewell.webp: one coherent Hooghly farewell composition, serene Bengali ekchala pratima in red sari and white shola ornament with all four children, natural human bearers supporting a bamboo frame, ghat spectators and water reflections. No floating detached idol or distorted sacred faces.

Composition constraints: landscape 1536x1024, no words/logos/watermarks, photographic lighting and natural scale, compassionate Bengali devotional character. Phone Saptami and Bhasan show the tableau above a dark reading area rather than cropping it to fill a narrow screen.

## Living homecoming redesign

The original artwork is preserved. The opening adds an original SVG alpona-inspired ornament and architectural framing. The journey renderer now layers the same images with lathed brass bells and lamps, instanced petals, a noise-based incense shader, localized river ripples, a synchronized dissolve and restrained dolly motion. Faces are not warped. Source people remain still artwork.

The new light mode uses each chapter's own background and devotional layers. Mobile framing preserves the full tableau above the reading area. See the current README and RESEARCH.md for the latest implementation and reproducible browser verification commands; the verification notes above describe earlier revisions.
